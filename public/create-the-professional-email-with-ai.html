00 The real problem

The email problem no one talks about

Most email guides tell you to "be concise" and "use a clear subject line." Helpful, but not why people hate writing emails. The real problem is figuring out what to say when the situation is even slightly awkward or high-stakes.

Chasing a late payment. Saying no to a client without losing them. Following up for the third time without sounding desperate. Telling a vendor their work isn't good enough. These situations paralyze people — not because they lack writing skills, but because the social calculus is hard.

AI is extraordinarily good at exactly this. Give it the context and the outcome you want, and it produces something far more diplomatically calibrated than most people would write under pressure.

01 The formula

The 4-part AI prompt formula for any email

You don't need to guess at how to prompt AI for emails. Every good email prompt contains four things — and when you include all four, the output almost never needs heavy editing.

The 4-part email prompt formula

🎯 Situation + 🤝 Relationship + 📌 Desired outcome + 🎨 Tone

Situation: What happened? Give the AI the relevant context — what the email is about, any relevant history, what prompted this message.

Relationship: Who is this person to you? A long-term client, a new supplier, a colleague you've never met, a customer who is upset?

Desired outcome: What do you want to happen after they read this? Pay the invoice? Schedule a call? Feel heard and stay a customer?

Tone: How should this feel? Professional and warm? Firm but respectful? Apologetic? Enthusiastic?

Full formula in action Write a professional email to a client I've worked with for two years. They haven't paid their invoice from 30 days ago and haven't responded to my first reminder. I want them to pay the invoice this week, or at least give me an update on timing. Tone: polite but firm — I value the relationship but need this resolved.

The shortcut

If you're in a hurry, even just dumping a messy description works: "I need to email my landlord about a leak that's been ignored for two weeks, I'm frustrated but want to stay professional." AI fills in the gaps. The formula just gets you better output faster.

02 Real scenarios

7 real email scenarios with ready-to-use prompts

Copy any of these prompts directly into Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini. Replace the bracketed parts with your specifics.

💰

Chasing a late invoice

The most dreaded email for most freelancers and small business owners

What people usually send

"Hi, just checking in on invoice #123 from last month. Please let me know when this will be paid. Thanks."

What AI helps you write

A message that states the facts clearly, maintains a professional tone, includes a soft deadline, and doesn't damage the relationship.

Ready-to-use prompt Write a polite but firm email to [client name], a [new/long-term] client, following up on invoice #[number] for $[amount], due [X days] ago. This is my [first/second] follow-up. I want them to pay within 5 business days or give me a clear timeline. Keep it professional — I want to preserve the relationship.
🙅

Saying no without losing the relationship

A client request you can't take on, a scope creep ask, or a meeting you can't do

Ready-to-use prompt Write a professional email declining [describe the request — extra work, a meeting, a discount] from [client/contact name]. I want to say no clearly but keep the relationship positive. Reason for declining: [your reason]. If appropriate, suggest an alternative or a future opportunity.
🤝

Cold outreach that doesn't sound cold

Reaching out to a potential client, partner, or collaborator you've never met

Ready-to-use prompt Write a short cold outreach email to [name/role] at [company]. I offer [what you do] and think it could help them with [specific problem or goal]. We've never spoken. Keep it under 100 words, lead with value not a pitch, and end with a low-pressure ask like a 15-minute call.
😤

Responding to an unhappy customer

A complaint, a bad review, or an upset message — turning frustration into loyalty

Ready-to-use prompt Write a professional response to an unhappy customer who [describe the issue — received a late order, had a bad experience, is asking for a refund]. The situation: [brief context]. I want to acknowledge their frustration genuinely, take responsibility where appropriate, and offer [solution or next step]. Tone: empathetic, calm, and solution-focused.
📅

Requesting a meeting or call

Asking for time from someone who doesn't know you — or hasn't responded yet

Ready-to-use prompt Write a concise email requesting a 20-minute call with [name], [their role]. Purpose of the call: [what you want to discuss]. Context: [how you know them or why you're reaching out]. Make it easy to say yes — suggest a couple of times or link to a scheduling tool.
✅

Post-meeting follow-up

The email most people forget to send — or send too generically to matter

Ready-to-use prompt Write a follow-up email after a meeting with [name] about [topic]. Key points we discussed: [bullet points]. Next steps agreed: [what each party will do]. I want to confirm the agreement, express genuine enthusiasm for working together, and make it easy for them to reply with any questions.
🔧

Giving critical feedback to a vendor or contractor

When work isn't up to standard and you need changes without an argument

Ready-to-use prompt Write a professional email to [vendor/contractor name] giving feedback that [describe the issue — work wasn't up to standard, missed a deadline, deliverable didn't match the brief]. Be specific about what needs to change. Tone: direct and professional, not passive-aggressive. I want them to understand the issue and fix it, not feel attacked.

Tone adjustment shortcut

After any generated email, you can instantly adjust: "Make this warmer," "make this more direct," "shorten it to 3 sentences," or "make it sound less formal." AI treats your first email as a draft — the real power is in the iteration, not the first output.

04 Before you hit send

5 rules before you send an AI-written email

AI-written emails are starting points, not final drafts. These five checks take 60 seconds and make the difference between an email that sounds like you and one that sounds like software.

RuleWhy it matters
Add one personal detailA specific reference to a recent conversation, shared context, or their situation makes any email feel human. AI can't know this — you have to add it.
Read it out loudIf you wouldn't say it in a conversation, don't send it. AI tends toward slightly formal phrasing — your ear catches what your eye misses.
Check the tone matches the relationshipAn email to a longtime client should feel warmer than one to a new contact. AI defaults to generic professional — adjust for the specific relationship.
Never paste sensitive information into consumer AIDon't include confidential client data, personal IDs, financial details, or proprietary info in prompts to free-tier AI tools.
Shorten it by 20%Research consistently shows shorter emails get higher response rates. AI emails tend to run long — cut the last paragraph if it restates what you already said.

The human judgment rule

The emails that matter most — apologies, sensitive negotiations, anything to a close professional relationship — should be AI-drafted but human-reviewed and edited. Use AI to overcome the blank screen, not to remove yourself from the process entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Will people be able to tell my emails are AI-written?

Only if you don't edit them. Raw AI output often has subtle tells — slightly formal phrasing, no personal details, polished to the point of feeling generic. The fix is simple: add one specific personal reference, read it aloud, and trim anything that sounds like a press release. After that, no one can tell.

Is it unprofessional to use AI for work emails?

No more than using spellcheck or email templates was unprofessional 10 years ago. Nearly half of B2B professionals already use AI to assist with email writing. What matters is the quality and authenticity of what you send — not what tools helped you get there.

Which is better for email — Claude or ChatGPT?

Both work well. Claude tends to handle nuanced, diplomatically sensitive emails slightly better — it follows detailed tone instructions more consistently. ChatGPT is faster for generating multiple variations quickly. Try both on free tiers and see which output matches your voice with less editing.

Can I use AI to reply to emails, not just write new ones?

Yes. Paste the email you received into Claude or ChatGPT, describe what you want to say and the tone, and ask it to draft a reply. Tools like Gemini in Gmail and Copilot in Outlook do this directly inside your inbox without copy-pasting.

What should I never put in an AI email prompt?

Avoid including confidential client data, passwords, financial account numbers, personal identification numbers, or proprietary company information in prompts to consumer AI tools (free or paid tiers). For sensitive corporate workflows, use enterprise plans that include data processing agreements.
